What is AstroNexus

Four challenges, one afternoon, and a school field turned into mission control.

AstroNexus is the first student-led space event of its kind in the UAE. Hosted by the Al Amal Space Club and the Physics Department at GEMS New Millennium School, it brings students from Grade 6 through Grade 12 — or the equivalent year in other curricula — into four parallel challenges — from decoding satellite telemetry to measuring sunspots against the size of Earth.

Each sub-event is pitched at a different age group and a different discipline: optics, rocketry, orbital mechanics, and solar research. Every one of them ends the same way — a team standing in front of judges, explaining what they built and why it works.

The Challenges

Ordered by year group. Each challenge runs in parallel on the day.

  1. 01 Grade 6 or equivalent

    LostInOrbit →

    Orbital crisis & satellite rescue. Decode telemetry, build debris shielding, and re-route severed power grids from mission control.
  2. 02 Grade 7–8 or equivalent

    TerraFab →

    Light-routing optics & interference. Steer laser beams around obstacles with front-surface mirrors and hit the target sensors clean.
  3. 03 Grade 9–10 or equivalent

    LaunchX →

    Rocketry design challenge. Design and build a model rocket with a randomly assigned partner, then fly it from the school field.
  4. 04 Grade 11–12 or equivalent

    HelioScale →

    Solar data analysis. Measure real sunspots in AstroImageJ, trace CME source regions, and scale them against the size of Earth.
